Life outside the lab and the codebase.

The CV covers the work. This is the rest of it: seven languages, a memory habit, a basketball court, and two cats who run the house.

Languages, and where that comes from

My father is a language scholar and has held positions at East China Normal University, UNC and UAlbany. Growing up around that shaped how I think about language, machine as well as human. It is part of why I care about controllability and interpretability in models rather than raw benchmark scores: I am interested in systems that communicate the way people actually do, and in being able to tell when they are only pretending to.

Mandarin is native and English is fluent. Spanish and French are intermediate. German, Japanese and Portuguese are beginner, and I keep chipping away at all of them. The quickest way to see what that is actually like is to play the guessing game, which is built out of the words that have caught me out.

Memorising things that do not need memorising

A long-running habit. I can still recite a shopping list from three weeks ago, which is a party trick with almost no practical value and I enjoy it anyway. The memory corner has both the speed drill and the palace method, including the cartoon flat where a salmon fills in a tax return.

Community, basketball and competitions

I have volunteered with my university's Cooperative Living Organization, spent time tutoring at language studios, and I still make time for basketball when I can. Hoopalytics started as an excuse to point data structures at something I actually watch.

Before all this, the thing I competed at was mathematics and physics:

  • AIME 12 of 15, global top 2.5 percent of 30,000, February 2024
  • AMC 12 Certificate of Distinction, top 5 percent globally, November 2023
  • Princeton University Physics Competition, 61st of 800 globally, December 2022
  • UKMT Senior Mathematical Challenge Gold, top 10 percent of 600,000, December 2022
  • British Physics Olympiad Top Gold, top 25 percent of 13,000, November 2021
