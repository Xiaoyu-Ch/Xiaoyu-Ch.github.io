<h2>Languages, and where that comes from</h2>

<p>
My father is a language scholar and has held positions at East China Normal
University, UNC and UAlbany. Growing up around that shaped how I think about language,
machine as well as human. It is part of why I care about controllability and
interpretability in models rather than raw benchmark scores: I am interested in
systems that communicate the way people actually do, and in being able to tell when
they are only pretending to.
</p>

<p>
Mandarin is native and English is fluent. Spanish and French are intermediate. German,
Japanese and Portuguese are beginner, and I keep chipping away at all of them. The
quickest way to see what that is actually like is to
<a href="{{ '/languages/' | relative_url }}">play the guessing game</a>, which is built
out of the words that have caught me out.
</p>

<h2>Memorising things that do not need memorising</h2>

<p>
A long-running habit. I can still recite a shopping list from three weeks ago, which
is a party trick with almost no practical value and I enjoy it anyway. The
<a href="{{ '/memory/' | relative_url }}">memory corner</a> has both the speed drill
and the palace method, including the cartoon flat where a salmon fills in a tax
return.
</p>

<h2>Community, basketball and competitions</h2>

<p>
I have volunteered with my university's Cooperative Living Organization, spent time
tutoring at language studios, and I still make time for basketball when I can.
Hoopalytics started as an excuse to point data structures at something I actually
watch.
</p>
