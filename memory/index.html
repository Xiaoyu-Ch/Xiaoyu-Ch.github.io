memory corner

A speed recall drill, and a cartoon apartment where a salmon does your taxes.

I have a long-running habit of memorising things that do not need memorising. Two of the techniques are below. The second one is why I can still list a shopping trip from three weeks ago.

Speed memory

Ten items. Seven seconds. Then write them down.

What to memorise

Press start. Ten items appear for seven seconds, then vanish. Type back as many as you can, in order if you can manage it. You get two scores: how many landed in the right position, and how many you got at all.

The memory palace

Five things to remember, parked in five rooms of a flat that does not exist. The images are absurd on purpose. Absurd is the whole mechanism: a cow on a sofa sticks where a carton of milk slides straight off.

    Now put it back

    Do not scroll up. Walk the flat in your head, room by room, and write down what you found in each one.

      Why the silly pictures work

      The method is old and the mechanism is dull: human memory is much better at places and pictures than at lists. So you stop storing the list and start storing a walk. You already know the layout of somewhere, so the order comes for free, and each room gives you one hook to hang something on.

      The images have to be ridiculous, because ordinary ones do not stick. A carton of milk on a sofa is forgettable. A cow surfing the sofa is not. The strangeness is the retrieval cue, and it is why every stop in the flat above is doing something it has no business doing.
