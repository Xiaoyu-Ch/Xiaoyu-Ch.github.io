research

What I work on, at the level a CV would put it.

Two questions keep showing up in my work. When a model or an agent looks like it is behaving, how do you tell whether it actually is? And when a result looks strong, how do you tell whether the benchmark is measuring the thing you think it is?

Scope note. This page stays at the level a CV would put it. The code for the ongoing safety work is not public, and unpublished results are not here. If you want detail beyond this, email me.

Current

AdaptGuard, sandbox policies for coding agents

OrderLab, UMich EECS · July 2026 to present

Undergraduate researcher, advised by Prof. Ryan Huang. I built an automated evaluation harness for agent sandbox policies and ran it first as a fifty-scenario pilot, then as a nine-hundred-scenario rerun across strictness levels.

The interesting part is that policies fail in both directions. A permissive policy let an agent fake a bug fix. A strict policy that blocked the cheat also blocked a legitimate fix. Aimed at OSDI.

  • Agent safety
  • Evaluation harnesses
  • Systems reliability

FinVault, an LLM agent safety benchmark

TEA Lab, UF ECE, Citi-sponsored · May 2026 to present

Research assistant. I own the data pipeline: single-pass logprob and entropy extraction on Qwen3-8B, and a 1,064-case run across 31 scenarios on dual H200 GPUs through SLURM.

Pipeline work is mostly debugging that nobody sees. I fixed silent CUDA corruption, an fp16 overflow and a label-leakage bug. The result I am most attached to is a negative one: a baseline scoring 0.987 AUROC turned out to be exploiting scenario identity rather than detecting anything.

  • LLM uncertainty
  • Benchmark validity
  • HPC and SLURM

VQ-VAE for medical dialogue

Zhongbo Zhichuang Technologies · September 2025 to present

Independent researcher. Discrete latent encoding applied to doctor and patient dialogue, for controllability and interpretability rather than raw fluency. Plus fifteen percent BLEU in this domain, presented as an oral at AICCONF 2026.

  • VQ-VAE
  • Controllable generation

Past

Perception for an autonomous harvesting robot

Computer Vision and Sensing Lab, UF ABE · February to August 2026

Undergraduate research assistant, advised by Dr. Henry Medeiros. I built the perception pipeline, depth estimation plus YOLO plus SAM3 segmentation, for a twenty-person autonomous bamboo-harvesting robot team. Over ninety-three percent accuracy on seventy seconds of field footage, trained on HiPerGator.

  • Computer vision
  • Robotics perception

Elo scoring for counter-speech preference data

University of Florida · May to July 2025

Human preference data for counter-speech is binary, which throws away most of the signal. I designed an Elo-based model converting binary preference labels into a continuous zero-to-ten scale, fed it into LLaMA-4 and DeepSeek fine-tuning pipelines, and annotated 450 dialogue responses for the CRetor dataset.

  • Preference modelling
  • Dataset construction

Where I want to take this

I am interested in PhD study and in industry roles in ML infrastructure and safety. The through line is reliability: building the measurement apparatus carefully enough that the result means what it appears to mean. A benchmark that is easy to score well on is usually broken, and finding out which is the part of the work I like most.
